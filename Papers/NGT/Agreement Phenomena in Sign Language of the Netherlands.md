#papers-ngt-grammar

The paper investigates **how grammatical agreement works in Sign Language of the Netherlands (NGT)**, particularly how verbs encode information about their arguments through spatial movement and handshape.

The main points are:
- **Two types of agreement:** Distinguishes location agreement (using positions in signing space) and gender agreement (using handshape classifiers).
- **Location agreement:** Verbs can move between spatial locations associated with referents, indicating relationships between subjects and objects.
- **Gender agreement:** Handshape can encode properties of a referent, such as its shape or semantic category.
- **Partial agreement:** Some verbs agree with both arguments, some with only one, and others show no agreement.
- **Person and number:** Argues that these are not the relevant grammatical agreement features in NGT; instead, location and gender are central.
- **Omitted arguments:** Subjects and objects can be left unexpressed when their referents are clear from context, even when the verb does not show agreement.
- **Main conclusion:** Agreement in NGT depends on verb semantics and spatial or handshape modifications, rather than following the agreement patterns familiar from many spoken languages.

Basically, every point in signing space can be used as a possible location, not only those on the horizontal plane.
