#papers-deaftech #papers-ethics

The paper explores **how technology could be designed around Deaf people's experiences, cultures, and desires**, rather than primarily treating deafness as a disability requiring technological solutions.

The main points are:
- **Deaf-centered design:** Criticizes technologies developed _for_ Deaf people without sufficiently involving Deaf people in defining their purpose.
- **Medical and audist perspectives:** Argues that Deaf technology is often framed around accessibility, assistance, and compensating for hearing loss.
- **Speculative workshops:** Deaf participants from different European countries imagined technologies on _Eyeth_, a fictional planet inhabited by Deaf people.
- **Alternative technologies:** Participants developed speculative technological concepts and social arrangements reflecting Deaf perspectives, interests, and ways of communicating.
- **Beyond accessibility:** Technology should support Deaf culture, identity, autonomy, and enjoyment, not only remove communication barriers.
- **Main conclusion:** Deaf people should have a central role in determining which technologies are developed and what problems those technologies aim to solve.
