#papers-ethics

The paper discusses **ethical and responsible development of sign language technologies**, questioning whether systems that are technically functional are necessarily appropriate or beneficial for Deaf communities.

The main points are:
- **Technical performance is not enough:** A system can achieve promising evaluation scores without being useful, reliable, or acceptable in real-world situations.
- **Deaf community involvement:** Deaf people should participate throughout the development process, including defining research goals and evaluating outcomes.
- **Risk of harm:** Inaccurate sign language recognition or translation can cause misunderstandings, particularly in high-stakes contexts.
- **Language and cultural diversity:** Sign languages are independent natural languages, and technologies should respect their linguistic complexity and variation.
- **Responsible evaluation:** Systems should be assessed for their real-world impact, not only through automatic performance metrics.
- **Main conclusion:** Researchers should question whether a technology should be developed and deployed, rather than assuming that technical feasibility makes it beneficial.
